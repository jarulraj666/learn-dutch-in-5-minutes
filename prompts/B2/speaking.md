## CEFR Level: B2 — Upper Intermediate | Skill: Speaking

The learner is upper intermediate. The goal is to speak fluently and spontaneously — arguing a position, handling unexpected questions, using varied vocabulary and complex sentence structures.

### Language Complexity Constraints (B2)
- Up to 25 words per dialogue line; complex multi-clause sentences
- All tenses; conditional mood (zou + infinitive); past perfect
- Wide vocabulary including collocations, idioms, and formal/informal register awareness
- Introduce at most 20 new items per episode
- Discourse markers for debate: "sterker nog", "integendeel", "toegegeven", "desondanks"

### Skill Focus: Speaking

The viewer practises fluent, nuanced speaking — making arguments, expressing doubt, qualifying statements, and repairing conversation. The dialogue should:

- Model advanced speaking strategies: qualifying ("Het klopt dat... maar toch..."), conceding ("Je hebt een punt, maar...")
- Include deliberate "thinking aloud" sequences where SpeakerB formulates a complex thought step by step
- "Spreek mij na:" drills focus on B2-level multi-clause sentence patterns and discourse phrases
- Teach how to structure a spoken argument: stelling (position) → onderbouwing (evidence) → conclusie
- Conversational repair and clarification at B2 level: "Wat ik eigenlijk bedoel is...", "Laat me dat anders formuleren"
- Quiz: viewer must complete or produce a B2-level argumentation phrase or qualify a statement
- Prefer `fill_gap` and `multiple_choice` quiz types

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Lines | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Introduction | ~8 | Introduce the debate/discussion topic; warm up with a controversial opinion |
| Core Dialogue | ~90 | Structured debate; complex arguments; hedging; qualification; clarification |
| Phrase Recap | ~40 | Drill argumentation phrases and complex sentence patterns |
| Quiz | ~53 | 5-line announcement + 8 × 6-line rounds; produce or complete argument structures |

**Total target: ~191 dialogue lines**

### Dialogue Style
- SpeakerA is a skilled debater; SpeakerB challenges, questions, and rebuts
- Include a note on pragmatics: how to be politely assertive in Dutch without sounding rude
- Teach the difference between "ik denk" (informal/tentative) and "ik ben van mening" (formal/confident)
