## CEFR Level: B1 — Intermediate | Skill: Speaking

The learner has solid basic Dutch. The goal is to hold conversations on familiar topics, express opinions, describe experiences, and explain plans with reasonable fluency.

### Language Complexity Constraints (B1)
- Maximum 18 words per dialogue line
- All major tenses: present, simple past, perfect, future (gaan + infinitive)
- Common 2000-word vocabulary; introduce at most 16 new items per episode
- Subordinate clauses, relative clauses ("die", "dat")
- Discourse markers: "eigenlijk", "nou", "trouwens", "dus", "want"
- Common collocations and fixed expressions

### Skill Focus: Speaking

The viewer practises expressing themselves in Dutch — opinions, experiences, plans, and disagreements. The dialogue should:

- Model how to express opinions: "Ik vind dat...", "Naar mijn mening...", "Ik denk dat het..."
- Include discussion turns where SpeakerA takes a position and SpeakerB politely agrees or disagrees
- "Spreek mij na:" drills focus on multi-clause sentences and opinion phrases
- Teach how to buy thinking time naturally: "Dat is een goede vraag...", "Laat me even nadenken..."
- Conversation repair strategies: "Bedoel je...?", "Kun je dat herhalen?"
- Quiz: SpeakerA gives a scenario or opinion prompt; viewer recalls the Dutch expression pattern
- Prefer `fill_gap` and `multiple_choice` quiz types

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Lines | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Introduction | ~10 | Introduce the speaking topic; activate existing vocabulary |
| Core Dialogue | ~80 | Discussion with opinion phrases, multi-clause sentences, natural fillers |
| Phrase Recap | ~44 | Drill opinion expressions and discussion patterns; Q&A pairs |
| Quiz | ~53 | 5-line announcement + 8 × 6-line rounds; produce or complete opinion/discussion phrases |

**Total target: ~187 dialogue lines**

### Dialogue Style
- SpeakerA and SpeakerB have a genuine back-and-forth discussion with mild disagreement and compromise
- Teach pragmatic competence: when to use formal "u" vs informal "jij/je"
- Include a grammar note on Dutch word order in subordinate clauses (verb at end)
