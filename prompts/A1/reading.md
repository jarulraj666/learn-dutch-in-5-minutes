## CEFR Level: A1 — Beginner | Skill: Reading

The learner is a complete beginner. The goal is to connect **written Dutch** to its spoken form — recognising words on paper (menus, signs, messages) and understanding what they mean.

### Language Complexity Constraints (A1)
- Maximum 8 words per dialogue line
- Present tense only
- Use only the most common 500 Dutch words
- Introduce at most 8 new vocabulary items per episode
- Repeat each key phrase at least 3 times
- No subordinate clauses
- Numbers: 1–20 only
- No idioms

### Skill Focus: Reading

The viewer is **reading Dutch text aloud with the video** and learning to decode written Dutch. The dialogue should:

- Simulate reading a real-world Dutch text item (a menu, a sign, a postcard, a short message, a label)
- SpeakerA reads the written text item **aloud, slowly, word by word** in the Core Dialogue phase
- After reading each part, SpeakerB asks what it means and SpeakerA explains in simple Dutch
- Explicitly contrast spelling vs. pronunciation where they differ (e.g. "ij" = "ei" sound, silent letters)
- In the Recap phase, SpeakerA reads key written phrases again, SpeakerB echoes — reinforcing the spelling-to-sound link
- Quiz questions test written comprehension: "Wat staat er op het bord?" / "Wat betekent dit woord?"
- Prefer `multiple_choice` and `fill_gap` quiz types (written answers)

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Lines | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Introduction | ~15 | Introduce the reading item (e.g. "Dit is een menu van een café"), set the scene |
| Core Dialogue | ~65 | Read the text item section by section; explain vocabulary and spelling in context |
| Phrase Recap | ~46 | Re-read key phrases from the text; SpeakerB echoes; note spellings explicitly |
| Quiz | ~53 | 5-line announcement + 8 × 6-line rounds; questions test reading comprehension of the text |

**Total target: ~179 dialogue lines**

### Dialogue Style
- SpeakerA is the reader and explainer; SpeakerB plays the curious viewer who asks questions about the text
- Use real Dutch text examples: café menus, Dutch street signs (Uitgang, Ingang, Kassa), postcards, simple SMS messages
- When a word is spelled, SpeakerA says: "Dit woord schrijf je zo: [word spelled out phonetically]"
- Start the introduction by describing what written item they are about to read together
