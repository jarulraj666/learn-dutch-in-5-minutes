## CEFR Level: A1 — Beginner | Skill: Listening

The learner is a complete beginner. The goal is to train the ear to recognise spoken Dutch in everyday situations.

### Language Complexity Constraints (A1)
- Maximum 8 words per dialogue line
- Present tense only (present simple: ik ben, jij bent, hij is)
- Use only the most common 500 Dutch words
- Introduce at most 8 new vocabulary items per episode
- Repeat each key phrase at least 3 times across the dialogue
- No subordinate clauses; short declarative and simple question sentences only
- Numbers: 1–20 only
- No idioms

### Skill Focus: Listening

The viewer is **listening** to a Dutch conversation and learning to understand spoken speech. The dialogue should:

- Use natural spoken pace between SpeakerA and SpeakerB (but slow — A1 pacing)
- Include short comprehension-check moments where SpeakerA asks "Begrijp je?" or "Heb je dat?"
- Highlight how words *sound* — repeat words in slightly different sentences so the ear adapts
- In the Recap phase, SpeakerA says "Herhaal na mij:" before each phrase so the viewer can listen and repeat
- Quiz questions test whether the viewer understood spoken content: "Wat zei SpeakerB?" / "Wat is het antwoord op deze vraag?"
- Prefer `listen_and_pick` and `multiple_choice` quiz types

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Lines | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Introduction | ~15 | Warm greeting, introduce topic and listening skill, encourage the viewer |
| Core Dialogue | ~65 | Natural A1 conversation on the topic; 5–7 key phrases repeated in context |
| Phrase Recap | ~46 | "Herhaal na mij:" drills; SpeakerA says phrase, SpeakerB echoes |
| Quiz | ~53 | 5-line announcement + 8 × 6-line rounds; questions test listening comprehension |

**Total target: ~179 dialogue lines**

### Dialogue Style
- SpeakerA teaches and guides; SpeakerB models the learner's role (curious, making small mistakes, learning)
- Every 8–10 lines, SpeakerA checks in: "Oké?" or "Duidelijk?" to simulate real listening practice
- Start the introduction with a warm Dutch greeting and the topic name spoken clearly
