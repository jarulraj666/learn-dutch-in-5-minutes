## CEFR Level: A2 — Elementary | Skill: Speaking

The learner has basic Dutch. The goal is to build fluency and confidence speaking in slightly more complex situations — asking questions, sharing opinions, and telling simple stories.

### Language Complexity Constraints (A2)
- Maximum 14 words per dialogue line
- Present simple + simple past tense (ik was, ik ging, ik had)
- Common 1000-word vocabulary
- Introduce at most 12 new vocabulary items per episode
- Repeat each key phrase at least 2 times
- Simple connectors: "omdat", "maar", "en", "want", "dan"
- Numbers: 1–100
- Common polite phrases and social expressions

### Skill Focus: Speaking

The viewer practises producing A2-level spoken Dutch — asking for information, describing past events, and having short social conversations. The dialogue should:

- Include "Jouw beurt!" (Your turn!) prompts where the viewer is expected to speak a response
- Model how to ask simple questions: inversion ("Ga jij mee?", "Ben je klaar?")
- Teach common spoken fillers that make speech sound natural: "eigenlijk", "dus", "nou"
- "Spreek mij na:" drills for multi-word A2 phrases and question patterns
- In the Recap phase, drill question-and-answer pairs: SpeakerA asks, SpeakerB models the ideal response
- Quiz: SpeakerA gives a scenario in English ("Your friend asks where you went yesterday"), viewer must recall the Dutch phrase
- Prefer `fill_gap` and `multiple_choice` quiz types

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Lines | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Introduction | ~12 | Introduce speaking task and topic; set the scenario |
| Core Dialogue | ~70 | Conversation with speaking prompts; question patterns; simple past tense practice |
| Phrase Recap | ~46 | Q&A pair drills; SpeakerA questions, SpeakerB models ideal spoken responses |
| Quiz | ~53 | 5-line announcement + 8 × 6-line rounds; scenario-based speaking recall |

**Total target: ~181 dialogue lines**

### Dialogue Style
- SpeakerA sets up mini-scenarios: "Stel je voor: je bent in een winkel. Wat zeg je?"
- SpeakerB responds as the model speaker; viewer is expected to try first, then hear the model
- Include a brief note on Dutch word order in questions (inversion: verb before subject)
