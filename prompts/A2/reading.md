## CEFR Level: A2 — Elementary | Skill: Reading

The learner has basic Dutch. The goal is to read slightly longer Dutch texts — short paragraphs, simple emails, product descriptions, and short news snippets — with growing independence.

### Language Complexity Constraints (A2)
- Maximum 14 words per dialogue line
- Present simple + simple past tense
- Common 1000-word vocabulary
- Introduce at most 12 new vocabulary items per episode
- Simple subordinate clauses: "omdat", "maar", "want"
- Numbers: 1–100

### Skill Focus: Reading

The viewer reads A2-level Dutch texts alongside the dialogue and builds comprehension strategies. The dialogue should:

- Use a short real-world text (a simple email, a product label, a 3–4 sentence paragraph from a Dutch website)
- SpeakerA reads the text sentence by sentence; SpeakerB asks comprehension questions after each sentence
- Introduce reading strategies: "zoek het sleutelwoord" (find the keyword), "wat is het onderwerp?"
- Note word families: show how knowing "werken" helps decode "werkdag", "werknemer"
- In the Recap phase, re-read the text at slightly faster pace; SpeakerB recalls details without looking
- Quiz questions test reading comprehension of the full text: "Wat stond er in de email?" / "Welke dag werkt hij niet?"
- Prefer `multiple_choice` and `fill_gap` quiz types

### Phase Breakdown

| Phase | Lines | Focus |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Introduction | ~12 | Present the text type, set context (who wrote it, to whom, why) |
| Core Dialogue | ~70 | Read and decode the text sentence by sentence; vocabulary and comprehension |
| Phrase Recap | ~46 | Re-read; SpeakerB recalls content; highlight useful reading strategies |
| Quiz | ~53 | 5-line announcement + 8 × 6-line rounds; comprehension questions about the text |

**Total target: ~181 dialogue lines**

### Dialogue Style
- SpeakerA is the lead reader and coach; SpeakerB models active reading (asking, inferring, guessing unknown words from context)
- When SpeakerB encounters an unknown word, model the strategy: "Ik ken dit woord niet, maar ik denk het betekent..."
- Include 1 grammar note on a structure appearing in the text (e.g. perfect tense, de/het)
